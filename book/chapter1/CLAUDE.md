# Chapter 1 — editing notes

These are deliberate choices. Don't "fix" them in a review.

## Structure

- **Evidence first, then my read.** "The evidence: what others have said" comes before my own argument on purpose: the reader first learns what current authors think, then what I think. Don't move it to the end or merge it into my read.
- **My read links back instead of restating.** When my read builds on a source, it says so in a short clause ("Khoo, above, says…"). Overlap between the two sections is intended. Keep it as a back-reference; don't delete either side.
- **Two labeled levels.** My read is split into *in practice* (the main target) and *in theory* (read charitably). Every argument sits under the level it belongs to. Don't mix them.
- **"What SOLID got right" stays.** The credit section is what makes the critique fair. Don't cut it to save length.
- **The running example repeats on purpose.** The `IFoo` next to its only `Foo` comes back in several sections. It is the chapter's running example: the reader sees one case judged from several angles. Don't swap it for new examples or cut the repetitions.

## The chapter's two tests

The chapter defines two criteria that later chapters are judged against. Don't weaken or reword them away:

- **Actionable**: two reviewers applying a rule to the same code reach the same verdict.
- **Decisions vs ceremony**: remove the structure and ask what the reader lost.

## Claims and sources

- **First-person experience stays as written.** Universal claims about other people ("nobody", "everyone", "the whole industry") are hedged to my experience or tied to a linked source.
- **Quote only what was checked against the original.** If a point is only known through a summary, paraphrase it and link it; don't quote it. (Currently: Beck and Feathers are paraphrased. The two Henney claims rest on a summary of his talk.)

## Spelling

US spelling. Quotes keep their original spelling (North's "centre").

## Length

Shorter is good, but it isn't the only goal. Don't cut nuance, evidence or the credit section just to reduce word count.

## Links

Chapter 1 is the one place allowed to link forward into Chapters 2 and 3.
